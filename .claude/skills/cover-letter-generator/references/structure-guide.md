# Cover letter structure

Three parts, each doing one job. A cover letter that restates the resume in
paragraph form is wasted space — it should make an argument the resume's
bullet format can't.

## 1. Hook (1-2 sentences)
Not "I am writing to apply for X position." State the single strongest,
most specific reason this candidate fits this role — pull it from the
highest-scoring bullet `resume-profile-manager`'s selector surfaced for this
profile, or from the standout metric a recruiter-persona critique in
`resume-analyser` would catch first.

## 2. Body (2-3 short paragraphs)
Pick 2-3 of the JD's Tier 1 requirements (from `job-description-analyzer`)
and, for each, one real resume-backed proof point — not a list, an argument:
what the requirement actually demands, and the specific thing the candidate
did that satisfies it. Every claim here must already exist in the resume or
career bank; a cover letter is not a place to introduce a new unverified
achievement.

## 3. Close (1-2 sentences)
Direct, no "I look forward to hearing from you" filler, no restating
enthusiasm generically. State availability/next step plainly.

## Length
250-350 words. A cover letter longer than the resume it accompanies is a
tell that it's padded.

## After drafting: run humanizer

Cover letters are exactly the genre `humanizer`'s pattern list targets
hardest — "I am passionate about," "I am confident that my skills," rule-of-
three achievement lists, "I would welcome the opportunity" closers. Always
run the draft through `humanizer` before delivering; don't rely on avoiding
these phrases while drafting from memory.
