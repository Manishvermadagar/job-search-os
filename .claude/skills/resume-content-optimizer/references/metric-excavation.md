# Metric excavation

Sourced from nipungakkhar/ats_resume_optimizer's Phase 4 technique — the one
genuinely useful, concrete part of that repo (the rest of it is a self-graded
"100/100" claim with no real scoring behind it; see resume-analyser's
SKILL.md for why that matters).

## When to use this
Any bullet the user writes that describes an action with no number — "grew
the newsletter," "improved onboarding," "managed a budget." Don't just ask
"do you have a metric?" once and move on to a vague bullet if they say no —
work through the categories below before giving up on quantifying it.

## Question categories (ask 4-6 in one batched message, not one at a time)
- **Volume & scale**: How many users/customers/records/transactions did this
  touch? What was the size of the team/budget/system involved?
- **Before/after**: What was the number before your change, and after?
  (Even a rough before/after beats no number.)
- **Growth & revenue**: Did this move a revenue, cost, or conversion number?
  By how much, or what direction and rough magnitude?
- **Frequency & consistency**: How often did this happen (daily/weekly/per
  release)? Over what time period?
- **Recognition**: Was this called out in a review, adopted by other teams,
  cited as a benchmark internally?

## If the candidate doesn't know a number
1. Propose an estimate using a reasonable range for their role/industry and
   have them confirm or correct it — mark it "~" or "approximately" in the
   draft until confirmed. Never present an unconfirmed estimate as a bare
   number in a final bullet.
2. If no number is obtainable even as an estimate, fall back to **proxy
   scale language** instead of leaving the bullet vague:
   - Count: "across 4 product lines" instead of "across multiple products"
   - Frequency: "in twice-weekly releases" instead of "in frequent releases"
   - Scope: "spanning the full onboarding funnel" instead of "improving
     onboarding"
   - Comparative: "the fastest-growing channel that quarter" instead of "a
     growing channel"

## Hard guardrails
- Dates, titles, and company names are never estimated or adjusted to fit a
  target role — those are facts, not places for "excavation."
- An estimate the candidate hasn't confirmed does not go into a final
  resume. If they can't confirm it in the session, leave the bullet
  qualitative rather than ship an unverified number.
- Don't inflate seniority or scope to match a job posting — metric
  excavation finds real numbers that were left out, it doesn't invent scale
  that wasn't there.
